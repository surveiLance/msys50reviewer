Module 2.2 - The Problem of Business and IT Alignment

Strategic Management Outputs
  ●​ Strategic management process produces high-level business strategy
     defining long-term direction
  ●​ Business strategy
        ○​ Mission
        ○​ Vision and values
        ○​ Strategic goals and objectives
        ○​ Competitive & environmental analysis (e.g. SWOT)
        ○​ Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
  ●​ “Does it make sense to start information systems planning directly from
     the overall business strategy to achieve alignment?”
        ○​ Business Strategy is Inadequate
                ■​ Vague, unknown, or merely absent
                ■​ Rarely provides clear direction for IT
                ■​ Unstable and frequently changes
                ■​ Often requires non-reusable IT components

Business Strategy Problem #1
  ●​ Generic statements
        ○​ To be the leading ISP in the country
        ○​ To increase market share by 15% over the next three years
  ●​ Subject to different interpretations
  ●​ May not be entirely documented
  ●​ How are these useful to IT planning?

Business Strategy Problem #2
  ●​ Business strategy does not often contain specific suggestions regarding
     practical IT implementation
  ●​ Business-related targets and performance goals offer little guidance
  ●​ IT planners often forced to decide based on guesswork
  ●​ Little to no value as input to IT and EA

Business Strategy Problem #3
  ●​ Frequent changes in business strategy gives no solid basis for ITplanning
  ●​ Priorities could shift at any time
  ●​ Wreaks havoc on existing IT plans

Business Strategy Problem #4
  ●​ Many IT systems only beneficial in short term
  ●​ Systems lifespan complicates modifications and IT agility
         ○​ Current systems are assets, but may be liabilities in next strategy
  ●​ Isolated legacy systems could accumulate
  ●​ Focus on new systems for current strategy could lead to alignment trap
         ○​ Alignment Trap:
                ■​ hiring ppl n creating deps specific only for a certain goal /
                   system
                ■​ when it gets scrapped, they won’t get used anymore –
                   rendering them useless



What Needs to Happen
  ●​ Middle ground between business and IT
         ○​ Addresses concerns on both sides
         ○​ Understandable and valuable for both
  ●​ Business: explain value of IT decisions
  ●​ IT: provide actionable suggestions
  ●​ Should be valuable either in short- (<1-2 years) long- (3-5 years) and very
     long-term (>3-5 years)

Compositionality

  ●​ Example
        ○​ Understand different parts of a car and how they work together as a
           whole
                ■​ Breaking down things first into more manageable parts

Integration of Architectural Domains
  ●​ One way to deal with EA complexity
  ●​ Provides insight for those who deal with architectures
  ●​ Some integration problems cannot be solved
  ●​ Cannot eliminate integration problems
        ○​ Concepts and tools only make it easier to deal with issue
  ●​ Two typical integration problems shown next

Integration Problem Example #1
  ●​ Different modeling standards and languages (e.g. UML, BPMN) used for
     each architectural domain
  ●​ View of each domain developed by distinct stakeholders with their own
     concerns
  ●​ Challenge to do one-to-one mapping (alignment) of model elements from
     different domains
  ●​ Example:
        ○​ Different notations, even within IT: entities, relations vs. classes,
           objects
Integration Problem #2
  ●​ Integration of structural and behavioral models
        ○​ Structural models: fundamental components of the system
        ○​ Behavioral models: reaction to those components but can affect
            structural models as well
        ○​ Both models affect each other
               ■​ Ex: Changing the water pressure = affects the structural
                   integrity of the faucet (it may break), or the structural integrity
                   of the faucet may affect the output pressure of the water
  ●​ Example
        ○​ How are software components related to application functions?
        ○​ ERD and DFD: how are they related?

Choosing Architectural Domains
  ●​ Domain: area of knowledge, influence, or ownership
  ●​ Framework-dependent domains
  ●​ Traditional vs. Modern (i.e. Cloud)
  ●​ Could start with Business and IT → possibly break down further

Questions to Ask When Choosing Deciding on Domains
  ●​ Who are the major stakeholders? What would each one want to focus on?
  ●​ What are the priority areas of the organization?
  ●​ What are the capabilities of the organization? What are the strengths and
     weaknesses of the organization?
  ●​ Is the preferred architectural approach flexible enough to freely allow
     choice of domains?
  ●​ How easy is it to integrate the different chosen domains?



Module 3.2 - Describing Enterprise Architecture

Capturing the Essence
  ●​ Essence of each domain is of more value to EA
  ●​ Focus on relations and interactions between domains
  ●​ Use of models is indispensable

Model
  ●​ abstract and unambiguous conception of something in the real world
        ○​ minimize miscommunication
        ○​ you’re sending a message to the stakeholders – don’t want to be
            sending an ambiguous message
  ●​ uses formalized graphical or textual language
        ○​ keep the number of symbols low – don’t want to overwhelm
            stakeholders. they want to understand the models.
  ●​ Model’s rigor requires architects to be more meticulous
  ●​ difficult to capture everyone’s needs in single model

Semantic and Symbolic Models
  ●​ Symbolic model
        ○​ Represents architecture properties using symbols that refer to reality
        ○​ Uses formal syntactic structure and notation known as description
             language
        ○​ PROBLEM: can be technical for other groups of stakeholders to
             understand.
  ●​ Semantic model
        ○​ Represents one possible interpretation of a symbolic model
        ○​ MORE IMPORTANT IN EA: easier for stakeholders to understand since
             symbolic models can become too technical.
  ●​ UML focuses on symbolic model; ArchiMate emphasizes latter
