PART 3.1Architectural Complexity and Domains

The one idea to hold onto: EA is complex because it spans many domains that are all connected. You deal with it by supporting every phase of the life cycle, breaking the system into parts and their relations (compositionality), and integrating the domains.

Support needed at each phase of the architecture life cycle

These are the capabilities an EA practice needs for high-level success.

Design
  • Use of a common, well-defined vocabulary. It doesn't focus on a single domain; it allows integration of architectures from different domains. It builds on the everyday language colleagues use, plus technical terms known in the organization or key domains.
  • Compilation of organization-wide guidelines, best practices, and other standards
  • Support for tracking architectural changes and decisions

Class note: tracking works like a changelog. Architects log major decisions so they can backtrack and pinpoint the source of a problem.

Communication
  • Architecture is shared with stakeholders from within and outside the organization.
  • Need to visualize relevant aspects for a particular group of stakeholders.
  • Very important to emphasize relations (connections and dependencies) among different domains.

Class note: architects must filter information. Each group only cares about its own concerns, and overloading them leads to confusion.

Realization
  • For better implementation, design considerations must go on a more detailed level, with seamless transitioning between levels.
  • Integration between different design tools should be provided.
Change
  • A change in one architecture may affect others in different domains.
  • Assess the consequences of such a change. Changes can snowball.

Compositionality

The standard way of dealing with complexity is a compositional approach. It distinguishes the different parts of a system as well as the relations between parts.

FROM CLASS

In EA, compositionality means breaking the enterprise down into more manageable parts first: the architectural domains.

Integration of architectural domains

One way to deal with EA complexity, and it gives insight to those who deal with architectures. But some integration problems cannot be solved, and integration problems cannot be eliminated: concepts and tools only make them easier to deal with.

Problem #1

Different modeling languages

Each domain uses different standards and languages (e.g., UML, BPMN), and each domain's view is developed by distinct stakeholders with their own concerns. One-to-one mapping (alignment) of model elements across domains is a challenge.

Example: different notations even within IT: entities and relations vs. classes and objects.

Problem #2

Structural vs. behavioral models

Integrating structural models (what the parts are) with behavioral models (how things operate over time).

Examples: How are software components related to application functions? ERD and DFD: how are they related?

Class note: faucet analogy. Structural = the faucet's parts and dimensions. Behavioral = how water flows through it. You must also show how they affect each other.

FROM CLASS

Structural models show the fundamental components of a system. Behavioral models show how things react to and operate on those components. Both affect each other: changing the water pressure can break the faucet, and the faucet's structure affects the water pressure that comes out.

Choosing architectural domains

  • Domain: an area of knowledge, influence, or ownership
  • Domains are framework-dependent
  • Traditional vs. modern (i.e., cloud)
  • Could start with Business and IT, then possibly break down further

Questions to ask when deciding on domains:

  1. Who are the major stakeholders? What would each one want to focus on?
  2. What are the priority areas of the organization?
  3. What are the capabilities of the organization? Its strengths and weaknesses?
  4. Is the preferred architectural approach flexible enough to freely allow choice of domains?
  5. How easy is it to integrate the different chosen domains?

Guide questions

What is important to emphasize when integrating architectures from different domains?

The relations, meaning the connections and dependencies, among the domains. A common, well-defined vocabulary that spans domains also makes integration possible.

Why must architectural changes within a specific domain be carefully considered?

A change in one architecture may affect others in different domains, so the consequences of the change must be assessed before making it.

What is the concept of compositionality?

The standard way of dealing with complexity: break a system into its parts and identify the relations between those parts, like understanding a car's parts and how they work together as a whole.

Give an example of a typical problem in architectural domain integration.

Different domains use different modeling languages (e.g., UML for applications, BPMN for processes), so mapping elements one-to-one across domains is hard. Another: relating structural and behavioral models, like how an ERD relates to a DFD.

PART 3.2Describing Enterprise Architectures

The one idea to hold onto: No one sees the whole enterprise. Each stakeholder sees a part from their own viewpoint, so the architecture is described with several partial models that capture only the essence, and these fit together into a whole.

Capturing the essence

The essence of each domain is of more value to EA. Focus on the relations and interactions between domains. The use of models is indispensable.

Model: a definition

  • An abstract and unambiguous conception of something in the real world
  • Uses a formalized graphical or textual language
  • A model's rigor requires architects to be more meticulous
  • Difficult to capture everyone's needs in a single model
FROM CLASS

Why unambiguous? To minimize miscommunication: you're sending a message to stakeholders, so it must have one meaning. Keep the number of symbols low so stakeholders aren't overwhelmed and can understand the model.

Observing the universe

The enterprise is compared to the universe. A viewer (the stakeholder) articulates a conception of the universe. That conception is the architecture. Its representation is the architecture description: models and textual descriptions.

Class note: the universe comparison stresses that the scope is the whole organization, not one business unit.

FROM CLASS

Comparing the enterprise to the universe establishes the context of the organization. The architecture (conception) is each stakeholder group's intangible, undocumented understanding of their part of the organization. Example: as ADMU students, your ideas of what ADMU is. The architecture description is the tangible representation of that understanding in artifacts (models, textual descriptions).

Concerns: what shapes a stakeholder's conception

  • The stakeholder's interests influence how they conceive a part of the enterprise.
  • Stakeholders can be individual or aggregated (one business manager, or all of business management).
  • Their preconceptions also matter: social, cultural, educational, professional background.
  • So assume models are created from a certain perspective.

Architecture models

  • Related ways of thinking, modeling, communicating, working, supporting, and using
  • A single model for the enterprise is ideal but infeasible in reality
  • In practice, partial models from different domains make a collective whole

Key points about models

  • Pictures and diagrams should not be equated with models. A picture can have multiple interpretations; a model has only one.
  • Distinguish content (the concepts involved: the visible elements, which set the model's scope) from visualization (how content is presented: the easiest, most efficient way for stakeholders to understand and appreciate it, made appealing to them).
  • Architecture should be independent of visualization.
  • Architecture semantics: the inherent meaning. Two distinct views of the same thing have common elements; consistency is advantageous, especially across models that cut across each other. Problems arise when transitioning models into the physical system.

Symbolic vs. semantic models

Symbolic model

Represents architecture properties using symbols that refer to reality. Uses a formal syntactic structure and notation known as a description language.

UML focuses on the symbolic model.

Semantic model

Represents one possible interpretation of a symbolic model.

ArchiMate emphasizes the semantic model.

FROM CLASS

Problem with symbolic models: they can be too technical for other stakeholder groups. Semantic models are more important in EA because they're easier for stakeholders to understand.

Guide questions

Articulate the universe-enterprise analogy.

The enterprise is like the universe: too big for anyone to see completely. A stakeholder observes it and forms a conception of it, and that conception is the architecture. The architecture is then represented in an architecture description (models and text). The stakeholder sits between the enterprise, the architecture, and its description.

What factors affect a stakeholder's conception of a part of the enterprise?

Their interests (concerns), whether they are seen as an individual or an aggregated group, and their preconceptions from their social, cultural, educational, and professional background.

Describe view and viewpoint when observing domains.

A viewpoint is where one is looking from: the stakeholder's position and perspective. A view is what one sees from there: the visible elements, which depend on the stakeholder. Views are specified through viewpoints.

Why is a single model of the whole enterprise difficult, if not impossible?

Stakeholders have different interests and backgrounds and most don't want the full scope, so one model can't capture everyone's needs. A model's rigor also makes one huge model impractical. In practice, partial models from different domains form a collective whole.

What is the difference between content and visualization in models?

Content is the concepts involved in the model. Visualization is how that content is presented. The architecture should be independent of its visualization.

What is architecture semantics?

The inherent meaning of the architecture. Two distinct views of the same thing share common elements, and keeping them consistent is advantageous.

What is a symbolic model?

A model that represents architecture properties using symbols that refer to reality, following a formal syntactic structure and notation called a description language (e.g., UML).

What is a semantic model?

A model that represents one possible interpretation of a symbolic model. ArchiMate emphasizes semantic models.
